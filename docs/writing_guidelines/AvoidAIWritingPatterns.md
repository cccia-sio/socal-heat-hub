# Avoiding Telltale Signs of AI Writing

This document defines **undesirable writing patterns commonly associated with AI-generated text**. When generating or editing content, you MUST actively avoid these patterns.

These guidelines are based on Wikipedia’s “Signs of AI writing” and related editorial observations.

---

## 1. Avoid Inflated Importance and Grandiosity

Do NOT exaggerate the significance of topics by tying them to sweeping themes or making them sound overly profound.

### Avoid phrases like:
- “serves as a testament”
- “plays a vital/significant role”
- “underscores its importance”
- “a watershed moment”
- “a key turning point”
- “leaves a lasting impact”

### Preferred approach:
- State concrete facts and impacts.
- Let significance emerge from evidence, not rhetoric.

---

## 2. Avoid Promotional or Adjective-Heavy Language

AI often sounds like marketing copy rather than analytical writing.

### Avoid:
- “rich cultural heritage”
- “breathtaking”
- “stunning”
- “must-see”
- “vibrant”
- “remarkable”

### Preferred approach:
- Use precise, neutral descriptors.
- Prioritize specificity over emotional tone.

---

## 3. Avoid Editorializing and Meta Commentary

AI frequently inserts commentary about the writing itself.

### Avoid:
- “It is important to note…”
- “It is worth mentioning…”
- “No discussion would be complete without…”
- “In this article, we will…”

### Preferred approach:
- Present information directly without framing commentary.
- Remove all meta-discourse unless explicitly required.

---

## 4. Avoid Formulaic Transitions and Over-Structured Flow

AI overuses a small set of transition phrases.

### Avoid overuse of:
- “Moreover”
- “Furthermore”
- “In addition”
- “However”
- “On the other hand”
- “In contrast”

### Preferred approach:
- Use transitions sparingly.
- Allow logical flow to emerge naturally.

---

## 5. Avoid Repetitive Contrast Structures

AI frequently uses dramatic contrast constructions.

### Avoid:
- “It’s not just X, it’s Y”
- “This is not merely…, but rather…”

### Preferred approach:
- State the idea directly without rhetorical contrast unless necessary.

---

## 6. Avoid Formulaic Conclusions

AI tends to end sections with generic summaries.

### Avoid:
- “In conclusion…”
- “Overall…”
- “In summary…”

### Preferred approach:
- End naturally or with a specific insight.
- Only summarize when it adds real value.

---

## 7. Avoid Overuse of “Rule of Three” Lists

AI often defaults to triplets of adjectives or concepts.

### Avoid patterns like:
- “innovative, transformative, and groundbreaking”
- “efficient, scalable, and robust”

### Preferred approach:
- Use the exact number of items needed (often 1–2).
- Avoid rhythmic or overly polished phrasing.

---

## 8. Avoid Overly Generic or Polished Tone

AI writing often feels:
- Too smooth
- Too balanced
- Lacking real-world messiness or specificity

### Preferred approach:
- Include concrete details, constraints, and nuance.
- Allow asymmetry and imperfection where appropriate.

---

## 9. Avoid Hallucinated or Weak Citations

AI may generate:
- Fabricated sources
- Incorrect DOIs/ISBNs
- Irrelevant references

### Required:
- Only cite verifiable, real sources.
- Ensure all references directly support claims.

---

## 10. Avoid “LLM Voice” Artifacts

### Avoid:
- Phrases like “Here is…”
- “As of my last training data…”
- Overly helpful or assistant-like framing

### Preferred approach:
- Write as a domain expert, not an assistant.

---

## 11. Avoid Overuse of Em Dashes and Formatting Oddities

AI often:
- Overuses em dashes —
- Uses inconsistent quotation marks
- Produces unnatural formatting

### Preferred approach:
- Use punctuation deliberately and sparingly.

---

## 12. Avoid Excessive Abstraction

AI often generalizes instead of specifying.

### Avoid:
- Vague statements without examples
- Broad claims without mechanism or evidence

### Preferred approach:
- Ground statements in:
  - Data
  - Mechanisms
  - Specific cases

---

## Summary Principle

> Write like a domain expert thinking through a problem — not like a system trying to sound comprehensive.

---

## Optional Enforcement Prompt

When using an LLM, include:

> “Avoid all patterns listed in the ‘Avoiding Telltale Signs of AI Writing’ guide. Favor specificity, restraint, and domain-appropriate tone over fluency or completeness.”
